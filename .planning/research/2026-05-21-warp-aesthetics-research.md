*Companion to the 2026-05-20 technical research. Date: 2026-05-21.*

## TL;DR

Time stretching is **never neutral**. Every era of producers has either fought against stretching artifacts or embraced them — and the embrace is what built whole genres (jungle, chopped-and-screwed, vaporwave, hyperpop). Ableton's warp modes carry strong cultural fingerprints: **Beats mode** is the DAW-native sound of dnb stutter, IDM transient displacement, and footwork chop; **Tones mode** is the granular-tinged pitched-vocal smear of cloud rap and Burial; **Texture mode** is the modern digital descendant of Basinski's decaying tape; **Complex Pro** is the "stretched bridge" of every modern pop record. Re-Pitch is DJ Screw's turntable in a knob.

For Terrain, the brief is **not faithful Ableton emulation** but **Ableton-mode-shaped doors into Terrain's own coloured world**. The plugin already owns a sonic stack: signature granular engine, three tape machines (Studio / Cassette / Wire), Space reverb echo trails, parametric EQ, modulation engine. The warp modes should feel like *Terrain's interpretation* of those three Ableton archetypes — each one feeding naturally into the granular engine downstream, each one slightly more *characterful* than the Ableton original so producers reach for it when they want the artifact, not when they want the cleanest possible stretch.

The "no other granular has this" angle: **the chops ARE the warp markers, and shift-drag on a boundary is direct gestural time manipulation**. Per-chop pitch independence + per-chop warp mode + per-chop stretch makes the slicer a *compositional generator*, not just a playback grid. Combined with the granular feed loop, you have a sampler that turns any source into a personal language in minutes.

## 1. The Art of Time Stretching — A Brief Cultural History

Time stretching began as a **fidelity problem**: on tape and vinyl, pitch and speed were locked. Slowing tape down dropped pitch; pitching up sped time up. The whole 20th-century studio repertoire of intentional speed changes (Beatles' "Strawberry Fields" varispeed splice, Lee "Scratch" Perry's dub manipulation, hip-hop's pitched-down vocals on early rap records) was a **negotiation with that constraint** — accepting pitch shift as the cost of tempo change, or vice versa.

**Hardware sampler era — the artifact as voice (mid-1980s–early 1990s).** The E-MU SP-1200 (1987) had a hard 10-second sample limit at 26.04 kHz, 12-bit. Producers like Pete Rock, DJ Premier, and Large Professor pitched samples up to fit them in memory, then pitched them down on output — the SP-1200's anti-aliasing filter and 12-bit quantization gave hip-hop its signature grit. The Akai S950 (1988) was the **first Akai sampler to offer time stretching**, separating pitch from duration — but the 1988 algorithm was rudimentary and produced unique artifacts (granular tearing at extreme ratios, transient-doubling on drums) that became part of its signature.[^s950]

**Jungle / drum & bass — the Amen Break and timestretch as instrument (1992–1997).** The 6-second "Amen, Brother" break by The Winstons (1969) was sampled into thousands of jungle tracks. Producers like Photek, LTJ Bukem, Dillinja, and Optical used **EMU samplers** to chop, layer, and **timestretch the break at extreme ratios** — typically speeding it up from 136 BPM to 160-180 BPM for jungle, with the timestretch artifacts (granular crunch, smeared snares) becoming the *sound* of the genre.[^amen] Photek's "Ni-Ten-Ichi-Ryu" and "The Hidden Camera" are reference points — surgical chopping with the stretch artifacts proudly audible.

**Chopped and screwed — DJ Screw's tape-speed manipulation (1990s, Houston).** DJ Screw discovered that **dramatically lowering pitch via turntable** gave rap lyrics a mellow, narrative-heavy weight, slowing tempo to ~60-70 BPM.[^djscrew] No algorithm — just two copies of the same record played slow on twin Technics. This is the **purest Re-Pitch aesthetic**: pitch and time married, dropping together. It's the cultural origin of every "slowed and reverb" YouTube channel and TikTok edit today.

**Glitch / IDM — embracing the artifact (Aphex Twin, Squarepusher, Autechre, 1995–2005).** Aphex Twin and the Warp / Rephlex / Planet Mu axis treated timestretch artifacts as **first-class material**: micro-timing oddities, pitch-manipulated drum hits, deliberate aliasing, sample-rate destruction. Aphex would stretch and resample iteratively, generating new material each pass.[^aphex] Mike Paradinas's Planet Mu (founded 1995) became the home of "jungle breaks deformed into baroque melodicism strewn with circuit sparkle" — Lunatic Harness (1997) is the archetype.[^paradinas]

**Vaporwave / hauntology — slowed pop as cultural commentary (2010 onward).** Daniel Lopatin's Chuck Person's *Eccojams Vol. 1* (2010) "warped pop hooks awash in delay, reverb, and analog tape compression; looped, chopped, and shifted, often creating strange odd time grooves like a CD skipping through a pedal chain."[^eccojams] Macintosh Plus's *リサフランク420* (2011) slowed a Diana Ross record into vaporwave's foundational text. Mark Fisher's **hauntology** theory framed this as music of "lost futures" — the slowed pop song as a ghost of capitalism's failed promises.[^hauntology] Lopatin himself described it as "slow modes of listening in an otherwise fucked-up/hypersped society."

**Modern canon — Basinski, Burial, Tim Hecker.** William Basinski's *Disintegration Loops* (2002) recorded **physical tape literally falling apart as it played** — the ultimate "time stretching as decay" gesture. Burial's wordless vocal pads on *Untrue* (2007) used Ableton Simpler with extreme attack/release and looped microsections of late-90s R&B acapellas (Ray J for "Archangel").[^burial] Tim Hecker's *Ravedeath, 1972* (2011) layered manipulated pipe organ recordings into "warmth and beauty struggling to emerge from a fog of static."[^hecker]

**Hyperpop — hard-tuning + extreme stretch (2017+).** 100 gecs, SOPHIE, and the PC Music orbit pushed pitch shifting and time warping into **caricature territory**: hard-tune at 0ms retune speed, formant shifts of 7+ semitones, vocals time-stretched then re-pitched then re-stretched. The "Ableton sound" became visible as a *style*.[^hyperpop]

**Cloud rap / Drain Gang (2013+).** Whitearmor's productions for Yung Lean, Bladee, and Ecco2k pair **reverb-drenched chopped vocals** with stretched, pitched-down R&B and trance samples. Yung Lean "stretches and jams syllables into verses" — using warp as performance, not just correction.[^cloudrap]

**"This was made in Ableton" as a cultural marker.** By the mid-2010s, Complex Pro mode's particular formant-preserving stretch artifact became identifiable to trained ears on countless pop records — the "stretched bridge" trope, the AI-generated-vocal-sounding harmonized stack, the slightly-too-glassy texture of a chopped sample running at non-unity speed. The DAW left a fingerprint.

## 2. Specific Ableton Mode Signatures — What Producers Actually DO With Them

### Beats Mode
**Mechanism:** Slices at transient markers, plays each slice as a triggered grain with a **Transient Envelope** decay control. At 100, no fade — clicky/gated. At 0, long fade — smooths gaps left by tempo change.[^beats]

**Cultural footprint:**
- **DnB / jungle chop stutter** — Photek-style cut-up, Calibre / dBridge style edits. Slice an Amen break to 16, set Beats mode, push tempo from 136 to 174 — the gaps between slices and the transient-envelope decay become the rhythmic *content*.
- **Footwork chop edits** — Chicago footwork (RP Boo, DJ Rashad, Traxman) uses extreme transient-stutter at 160 BPM. **Jlin** takes this further into "mathematically intentional" chopping where a single vocal syllable is flickered "like a loose light fixture."[^jlin]
- **IDM transient displacement** — Aphex Twin / Squarepusher / Venetian Snares use Beats mode (or its hardware-sampler ancestors) to push transients off-grid, creating micro-timing oddities and "wrong" feels that are deliberate.
- **Transient-envelope abuse** — pulling the envelope to 0 turns Beats into a gate effect; pulling it to 100 with extreme stretch reveals the gaps as silence-clicks, which is itself a signature.

[^beats]: [Audio Clips, Tempo, and Warping — Ableton Manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/audio-clips-tempo-and-warping/); [Ableton Live: Warping Revisited — SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/ableton-live-warping-revisited).
[^jlin]: [Jlin's Black Origami Gives New Shape to Footwork — Village Voice](https://www.villagevoice.com/2017/05/31/jlins-black-origami-gives-new-shape-to-footwork/).

### Tones Mode
**Mechanism:** Small-grain granular stretching with adjustable grain size, optimised for monophonic pitched material.

**Cultural footprint:**
- **Pitched-vocal lines** — the entire cloud rap / trap melodic ad-lib aesthetic (Future, Travis Scott, Bladee) of pitched-up "girl voice" chops sits adjacent to this mode.
- **Burial's wordless vocal pads** — *Untrue* used what is effectively Tones-mode-style stretching of late-90s R&B acapellas. The grain artifact at slow stretches creates that distinctive choked, half-recognizable melodic texture.
- **Lo-fi melody stretching** — slowing a sampled keys/Wurlitzer line by 1.5x in Tones mode introduces the granular gentleness that "lo-fi hip-hop" producers (Nujabes lineage, Mac DeMarco-ish bedroom pop) rely on as a sonic glue.
- **Monophonic instrument retuning** — guitarists and bassists use Tones to fix off-tempo recordings without the metallic ringing of bad pitch correction.

### Texture Mode
**Mechanism:** Granular stretching with **Flux** parameter that randomises grain reading positions, deliberately introducing chaos. Trades pitch accuracy for textural richness.

**Cultural footprint:**
- **Ambient pad stretching** — Tim Hecker (*Ravedeath, 1972* organ recordings), Stars of the Lid, Loscil — Texture mode is the digital descendant of Basinski's decaying loops. Push grain size up + Flux up = ambient blanket.
- **Field-recording manipulation** — Jana Winderen, Chris Watson, modern noise / dark ambient producers (The Caretaker's *Everywhere at the End of Time* is the canonical reference for digital decay aesthetics).
- **Drone composition** — Oneohtrix Point Never's early work (*Returnal*, *Replica*) heavily uses granular stretching of source material into static drones.
- **Long-tail tail-stretching** — a producer takes a 2-second pad note, Texture-stretches it to 30 seconds for a section transition. Flux at ~50% makes it shimmer, Flux at 100% makes it crystalline-shattered.

### Complex / Complex Pro
**Mechanism:** Phase vocoder with Complex Pro adding **Formants** (vocal-character preservation under pitch shift) and **Envelope** (spectral envelope size control). Higher CPU, "best for full songs and vocals."[^complex]

**Cultural footprint:**
- **The "stretched bridge"** — a ubiquitous modern pop trope where the bridge of a song is stretched to half-speed with Complex Pro, creating that glassy, slightly-otherworldly "AI vocal harmoniser" sheen.
- **Full-mix stretching** — remixers stretching whole 4-bar loops to new tempos without retuning the bassline. Almost every commercial remix produced after 2012 has this signature somewhere.
- **Formant-locked harmony generation** — pitch a vocal up/down a 3rd / 5th, formant-lock to preserve identity, layer back under the original. Common in pop, K-pop, modern country.
- **Vocal time-stretch for melodic content** — pull a single sustained vowel out to 4x length, becomes a vocal pad.

[^complex]: [Complex Pro Warp Mode — PCAudioLabs](https://pcaudiolabs.com/ableton-live-warping-part-8-how-to-use-complex-pro-warp-mode-in-ableton-live/).

### Re-Pitch
**Mechanism:** Pitch and tempo locked together — exactly like tape varispeed or a turntable's pitch slider.

**Cultural footprint:**
- **DJ Screw / chopped-and-screwed** — the foundational use case. Whole songs pitched down 25–35% for that mellow, narrative weight.
- **TikTok "slowed and reverb"** — Slater's 2017 viral slowed Lil Uzi Vert remix kicked off the modern wave; entire YouTube channels now exist exclusively for slowed-and-reverb edits.[^slowed]
- **Nightcore** — the opposite gesture (pitched up + sped up), originating in Norway 2002, dominant on early 2010s YouTube anime fandoms.
- **Vinyl-deck pitch-down trope** — drop-into-half-time DJ transitions, the classic "record slowing down" deceleration that ends countless dance tracks.

[^slowed]: [The History of Slowed + Reverb Music — Sottovoce DSP](https://sottovocedsp.com/blogs/blog/the-history-of-slowed-reverb-music); [Nightcore and Slowed & Reverb Remixes — Chartmetric](https://hmc.chartmetric.com/nightcore-slowed-reverb-tiktok-remix/).

## 3. What Makes a Time Stretcher Feel "Iconic"

Every stretcher has a fingerprint. Trained ears can identify these — and **the fingerprints define eras**.

- **Ableton Beats mode — gated decay + audible silence-clicks at extreme stretch.** The Transient Envelope curve has a recognisable shape: a fast attack, a programmable decay that feels *slightly* longer than you'd expect, then silence. At extreme stretches the gaps become content. This is the DAW sound of "I sliced a breakbeat and pushed the tempo around."
- **Ableton Tones mode — grain-size-dependent shimmer.** Small grains = fluttering / "speaking from underwater" character. Large grains = smearing into pad-territory. Burial's vocal pads sit in this grain-size sweet spot at ~50-100ms.
- **Ableton Texture mode — Flux-driven shimmer / crystallisation.** Flux = 0 is smooth granular; Flux > 50 introduces a "moth-wing flutter" that's identifiable as the Texture-mode sound.
- **Ableton Complex Pro — phasey glassiness.** The phase vocoder under the hood produces a particular **spectral phasiness** — described as "gravelly, grainy, phasey sound on voices" — that's the hallmark of polyphonic stretching.[^elastique] At mild ratios (0.9x–1.1x) it's nearly invisible; at extreme ratios it becomes a recognisable colour.
- **zplane élastique (Cubase, Studio One, Bitwig, many others) — phasey shimmer at low ratios; metallic ringing on percussives.** The default-when-you-don't-know-better stretcher; produces decent results on most material but never has a *signature* — its tell is the absence of personality.[^elastique]
- **Rubberband R3 (open-source, used in MuseScore, Mixxx, others) — smoother smear than élastique on vocals and bass, but heavier transient smearing on drums.** "Higher-quality results than R2 for most material, especially complex mixes, vocals and other sounds that have soft onsets."[^rubberband]
- **SoundTouch (Audacity, many DJ tools) — metallic ringing on stretched sustained tones.** Recognisable shimmery / glassy artefact at extreme ratios; "audiobook narrator" cleanliness at mild ones.
- **Phase vocoder generally — "transient smearing" + "phasiness" / "loss of presence."** Universal academic-known artifacts.[^pv]
- **Granular generally — grain-boundary clicks if window is wrong.** "Short grains under 20 ms produce clicky, glitchy textures that sound almost percussive."[^grain] These clicks become **character** when intentional, **broken** when not.
- **Tape varispeed — pitch-locked-to-time, slight wow/flutter, head-bump frequency colouration.** The reference everyone secretly chases. Terrain's three tape machines already model this.
- **Signalsmith Stretch (the library we picked) — phase-locking-based reduction of phasiness, intentionally lower-CPU than R3.** Its tell is **clean preservation of stereo image and pitch under moderate ratios**; at extreme ratios it tends toward a "softer" smear than élastique.

## 4. What Time Stretching MEANS Musically — Beyond Mechanics

Opinionated take, concrete:

1. **Decoupling tempo from pitch** is the obvious one. The least interesting use. Everyone has this. Tape varispeed used to bind them; modern algorithms unbind them. This is *table stakes*, not the point.

2. **Lengthening sustains creates contemplative space.** Stretching a 2-second note to 30 seconds turns rhythmic music into ambient music. Basinski, Hecker, Stars of the Lid, Lopatin's early work — all built careers on this single gesture. The note that was a punctuation becomes a *room*.

3. **Compressing material to dense rhythmic information.** Inverse of #2 — taking long material and stretching it *shorter* turns chord progressions into glitch-drums. Jlin's footwork lives here. So does breakcore.

4. **Imprinting a "processed" identity on clean source.** A clean vocal stretched even 5% in Complex Pro **sounds processed** — it carries an "I have been touched by software" signature. This is sometimes desirable (modernity, polish, "produced") and sometimes a betrayal (you wanted the source to feel raw). The choice of *which stretcher* signals which.

5. **Creating uncanny half-recognizable familiarity.** The "slowed-down song" effect — you recognise the source but it has been *defamiliarised*. This is hauntology's whole project: making the familiar carry the weight of loss. Vaporwave, chopped-and-screwed, modern slowed-and-reverb TikTok edits all live here.

6. **As a compositional generator — stretched audio becomes new musical material.** This is the most important one for an instrument like Terrain. You cannot **compose** a Basinski piece — you have to **discover** it by stretching and listening. The stretcher becomes a *partner*. The producer's job is curating what the stretcher produces, not writing it.

7. **As textural transformation — drum becomes pad, vocal becomes string.** A snare hit at 16x with proper grain windowing becomes a sustained breathy pad. A spoken phrase at 8x becomes a string section. **Category-violation** of the source material's identity is the whole game. This is where granular and warp meet — and where Terrain has natural advantage over most plugins because the granular engine downstream amplifies the effect.

## 5. How Terrain Can Differentiate Within This Lineage

Terrain already owns: signature granular engine, three tape machines, Space reverb echo trails, parametric EQ, modulation engine, terrain mesh viz, Lunar Haze themes.

**The warp modes should not be a separate processing block — they should feel like a continuation of Terrain's existing voice.**

### 5a. Relationship to the existing tape FX stack
The three tape machines (Studio / Cassette / Wire) already give Terrain a strong **temporal-instability identity** — wow/flutter, IPS-baked-in speed modulation, hiss, saturation. The warp modes should sit **upstream** of tape (warp first, then tape adds analog character on top of warped material). This is important because:
- Warp artifacts (Beats-mode silence clicks, Tones-mode grain shimmer, Texture-mode flux flutter) **feed naturally into Cassette / Wire's wow/flutter and saturation**, blurring the line between "digital warp artifact" and "analog tape artifact." That ambiguity is the **aesthetic prize**: Terrain's stretched material sounds *neither obviously digital nor obviously analog*, but a hybrid only Terrain produces.
- Studio mode (clean tape) should let warp artifacts speak clearly when wanted.
- Cassette and Wire should mask warp artifacts in the most musical way when the producer wants stretch-as-secret.

### 5b. Feeding warp into the granular engine
**This is the differentiator vs. any competitor.** A chop with Warp = Texture, Stretch = 4x, sent into the granular engine with feed-to-grain enabled, creates a **second-order textural transformation**: warp first textures the source, then granular fragments the textured result. This is **not a chain Ableton can do natively** — Ableton's warp lives on the clip, the granular processing would have to be in a separate plugin downstream, and the loop-back is awkward. Terrain integrates them.

Concrete consequence: **the granular engine sees pre-warped material as its source buffer**, and the warp's artifacts become grain content. This is the "no other granular has this" specific angle the user articulated.

### 5c. Positioning vs. competitors
- **Output Portal** — preset-heavy, focused on retaining source character. Great at "drum becomes alien." Terrain should be more *characterful and tape-coloured* and more **gestural** (the chop-as-warp-marker, the shift-drag boundary stretch).[^portal]
- **NI Granulator II / Granulator 3** — free / cheap, deeply granular but no sampler workflow around it, no warp paradigm. Terrain wraps granular in an Ableton-Simpler-like workflow that's familiar to Ableton producers.
- **Tonster Atlas** — drum-loop sample-explorer with AI tagging. Different category.
- **Magic Death Eye Stutter** — pure stutter/chop FX, no sampler context.
- **Imaginando BAM** — beat-aware granular, drum-focused. Terrain is broader-purpose.
- **FXpansion / ROLI Cypher2** — granular-capable synth, not sampler.

**The gap Terrain fills:** Ableton-Simpler-quality sampler workflow + per-chop warp modes + per-chop pitch + signature granular feed loop + tape character on top. **A Max-for-Live device's worth of integrated tools shipped as a single plugin** — directly aligned with the user's stated north star.

### 5d. Faithful, caricature, or distinct?

**Recommendation: caricature, with one distinct mode.**

- **BEATS** — Ableton-shaped but with **Cassette/Wire flavour optional**: Terrain's BEATS should sound like Ableton's Beats mode if the tape stack is bypassed, but enabling Cassette flavour adds a subtle tape-edge softness to the transient envelopes that's identifiable as *Terrain's* Beats. Don't try to be cleaner than Ableton. Be *gritter and more characterful*.
- **TONES** — extend Ableton's grain-size paradigm but expose the grain size and shape as **modulatable** via the LFO/XY pad. Ableton's Tones is a fixed character; Terrain's TONES should *move* under modulation.
- **TEXTURE** — push Flux harder than Ableton. Where Ableton's Texture stays musical, Terrain's TEXTURE should be willing to **break beautifully** — embrace the grain-boundary character as feature.
- **One distinct mode (no Ableton analog) — call it WARP or DECAY or similar.** This is the "Basinski door": a mode that treats stretched material as **deteriorating physically over time** — grain density drops, high-frequency content degrades, spectral noise accumulates. Implementation: stretched material slowly degrades on extended sustain. **This is the mode no other granular has, and it ties directly to the user's "Basinski as sonic north star" vision** (recorded in MEMORY.md, 2026-05-20).

Skip Complex Pro emulation. It's expensive to do well and the cultural use case (polish on full mixes) is not what Terrain is for. Re-Pitch is trivial — keep it but don't push it.

## 6. Failure Modes — What Warp Aesthetics to AVOID

The bar for "good artifact" vs. "bad artifact" is essentially: **does the artifact feel intended?**

- **Over-pristine "audiobook narrator" stretching.** élastique at moderate ratios with default settings on speech. Sounds *fine*. Sounds like nothing. The biggest sin for an artifact-forward plugin is being indistinguishable from a transparent stretcher. **Terrain's warp must always have a discernible character even at 1.0x stretch.**
- **Muddy phase-vocoder smear.** Bad implementations of Complex-style stretching produce a "loss of presence" / "underwater" smear that **sounds broken, not interesting**. The line between "characterfully phasey" and "broken-sounding" is narrow. Signalsmith Stretch's phase-locking helps here.
- **Cliché dnb-stutter that's been done to death.** Every plugin that does beat-slicing with transient-envelope decay risks sounding like "preset 47: 1996 jungle break." Terrain's BEATS should have a *sweet spot range* that's recognisable as Terrain, not Ableton-Beats-emulation.
- **Over-loud grain boundary clicks at low stretch ratios.** Clicks are great content when **intended at extreme stretch**; at 1.05x they're broken. Terrain needs careful grain-envelope design to avoid clicks emerging where they shouldn't.[^grain]
- **Transient doubling on drums.** Bad phase-vocoder implementations produce ghost-transients ahead of and behind the real transient (the SELEBI paper's whole motivation). For BEATS mode, transient location must be preserved with sub-millisecond accuracy.
- **Formant-broken vocals on pitch shift.** Pitching vocals without formant compensation makes them sound like chipmunks; pitching with bad formant compensation makes them sound like dolls. Terrain's TONES should expose formant control on vocal material.
- **CPU spike on warp activation.** Switching warp modes mid-playback shouldn't introduce a glitch. Pre-warm both algorithms or crossfade between them.
- **Pitch drift on extended sustain.** Some granular stretchers wander pitch when stretched 10x+. Phase-locking prevents this.
- **Stereo image collapse on Tones/Complex stretching.** Bad phase vocoders narrow the stereo field. Test on stereo source material.
- **"Plugin demo" sound** — the slightly-too-clean, slightly-too-impressive sound of an algorithm being shown off. Terrain should sound like a producer's *tool*, not a vendor's *demo*.

The rule: **if a sober producer with critical ears can't tell whether they intended an artifact or it just happened, the artifact is bad. If they can tell — and they like it — it's good.**

## 7. Concrete Sonic Targets for v1.0

Seven "if a user does X, they should hear Y" targets to guide DSP tuning. These are the ear-test pass criteria for the warp implementation.

1. **Photek-style jungle chop.**
   User drops a 120 BPM Amen-break loop, slices to 16 chops, sets all to **BEATS** with stretch=1.45x (to push to ~174 BPM dnb). Should sound like cut-up jungle with audible-but-musical transient envelope decay. **NOT** muddy phase-vocoder smear, **NOT** doubled transients, **NOT** suspiciously clean. Reference: Photek "Ni-Ten-Ichi-Ryu" snare cuts.

2. **Burial vocal pad.**
   User loads a wordless R&B-style vocal acapella sample, slices into 4 chops, sets one chop to **TONES** mode with stretch=4.0x and small grain size. Should sound like a Burial *Untrue*-era vocal pad — chocked, half-recognizable, granular-tinged. **NOT** chipmunk pitched-up, **NOT** glassy Complex-Pro-sheen.

## Closing Note — The Bigger Frame

The user's vision recorded in MEMORY.md is "essentially bringing Ableton, we're bringing a Max for a Live device into the plugin world." The warp implementation is the **load-bearing pillar of that vision** because:

- Slicer + per-chop warp + granular feed is the **Simpler→granular pipeline** Ableton users already build manually with Drum Racks and M4L devices.
- Doing it inside one plugin, with shared tape/space/EQ character downstream, **collapses what would be a 5-device chain into a single instrument**.
- The cultural inheritance (jungle, chopped-and-screwed, vaporwave, hauntology, cloud rap, hyperpop, ambient/drone) gives the warp implementation a **century of reference points** — producers will *immediately* understand what to do with it because the gestures (chop, stretch, pitch, slow, decay) are deeply established musical vocabulary.

The job is not to reinvent stretching. The job is to **give producers Ableton-shaped gestures in a Terrain-coloured world**, with one move (the DECAY/WARP mode + granular feedback) that Ableton itself cannot make.
