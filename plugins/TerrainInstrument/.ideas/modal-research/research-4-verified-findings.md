SUMMARY: The verified primary literature fully specifies a real-time per-voice physical modeling engine: plucked/struck strings run as single-delay-loop (EKS) models costing on the order of one oscillator (fractional-delay allpass + a two-parameter one-pole loop filter), piano stiffness becomes a live knob via closed-form Thiran dispersion cascades (~20 biquads suffice for a low note tuned to 6 kHz), body resonance comes nearly free through commuted synthesis (body IR preconvolved into the excitation wavetable), and struck/mallet timbres run as 3-multiply-per-mode reson banks where hundreds of modes are trivially affordable and strike position is a pure gain-reweight. Sustained families (bow, reed, air-jet, lip) all reduce to the McIntyre-Schumacher-Woodhouse pattern — one nonlinear table inside the same resonator loop — so a single architecture covers piano through trumpet by swapping only the nonlinearity F, the reflection/loop filter, and parameters. FDTD is confirmed as the most general method but numerically fragile and not the per-voice real-time path, and the waveguide efficiency advantage provably vanishes for 2D/3D membranes/plates, so drums/bells/plates should be modal. Verified realism levers map cleanly onto the requested 10-knob design: lowpassed-noise excitation as velocity-to-brightness, dual-polarization mistuning for natural beating and two-stage decay, strike-position gain maps, live inharmonicity B, and the perceptually validated one-scalar material law d_n = ρ·f_n. The final finding assembles these into the recommended hybrid exciter → (dual-SDL string | MSW loop | modal bank) → commuted-body engine with a 5 ESSENTIAL + 5 SCULPT split and per-family recipes; the one real gap is that commercial teardown (Pianoteq/SWAM/Chromaphone/Sculpture), open-source repo mining, and UI-presentation claims did not survive adversarial verification.

## FINDING 2 [high | 3-0 ×3 (merged claims 3+5+16)]
Karplus-Strong is exactly an N-sample delay loop initialized with random numbers ('white noise') whose loop filter is the two-point average y(n)=[x(n)+x(n-1)]/2 — the special case of the single-delay-loop model with all losses and dispersion lumped at one point in the round trip. Pitch = fs/N, fine-tuned by interpolation; total computational cost is on the order of ONE oscillator yet it produces an entire harmonic series — the cheapest plucked-string recipe available. Crucially for realism, in practice the noise burst is lowpass filtered and the cutoff frequency acts as a dynamic-level control (velocity → brightness), because real strings are brighter at louder dynamics — a ready-made, verified noise-in-excitation technique.
SOURCES: https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/ ; http://users.spa.aalto.fi/vpv/publications/cmj98.pdf ; https://www2.ph.ed.ac.uk/~sbilbao/0470510463.pdf
EVIDENCE: Three independent primary sources verified verbatim: PASP Karplus_Strong_Algorithm.html ('lowpass cut-off frequency gives an effective dynamic level control since natural stringed instruments are typically brighter at louder dynamic levels'); Karjalainen-Välimäki-Tolonen CMJ 22(3) 1998 ('losses and the dispersion are lumped at a single point in the round trip'; KS = SDL special case with excitation as initial delay-line state); Bilbao 2009 Ch.1 ('computational cost is on the order of that of a single oscillator, yet ... yields an entire harmonic series'). Minor nuance: exact KS pitch with the 2-point averager is fs/(N+1/2), covered by the interpolation clause.

## FINDING 3 [high | 3-0 ×2 (merged claims 4+6)]
The production string voice is the single-delay-loop (SDL) reduction: for linear time-invariant strings the full bidirectional digital waveguide provably collapses to one delay loop, needing only (a) a fractional-delay filter — first-order allpass or Lagrange interpolation — for continuously variable pitch, and (b) a controllable loop filter for frequency-dependent decay, the popular choice being a one-pole lowpass with exactly two parameters: DC gain (overall decay time) and cutoff (brightness/damping slope). Bidirectional waveguide structures are only required when the string carries essential nonlinearities or time-varying behavior (e.g., a hammer or bow acting at a point on the string).
SOURCES: http://users.spa.aalto.fi/vpv/publications/cmj98.pdf
EVIDENCE: Verified verbatim in the peer-reviewed Computer Music Journal 22(3) 1998 paper (Karjalainen/Välimäki/Tolonen), which derives the bidirectional→SDL equivalence in detail: 'a single fractional delay filter for fine-tuning the pitch is sufficient ... One popular choice is a one-pole digital loop filter with two parameters: a DC gain and a cutoff frequency parameter (Jaffe and Smith 1983).' Verifier noted one later refinement: tension-modulation nonlinearity can be approximated inside an SDL via a time-varying fractional delay (Tolonen et al. 2000), so the bidirectional requirement applies mainly to point-interaction nonlinearities (hammer felt, bow friction).

## FINDING 4 [high | 3-0 ×3 (merged claims 1+2+7)]
Commuted synthesis is the verified route to instrument-body realism at near-zero runtime cost: because string and body are approximately linear and time-invariant, the body resonator commutes with the string, so the body impulse response is preconvolved (offline) into the pluck excitation and stored as a single aggregate excitation wavetable that feeds the string loop — 'the body filter is replaced by an inexpensive lookup table.' This eliminates the single most expensive per-voice component (a very high-order body filter) and is the key CPU trick for acoustic guitar, lute, and harp bodies (and piano soundboards via commuted-piano adaptations) under a Serum-level budget.
SOURCES: https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/ ; http://users.spa.aalto.fi/vpv/publications/cmj98.pdf
EVIDENCE: PASP Commuted_Synthesis.html verified verbatim ('we may commute the string and resonator ... convolved with the resonator impulse response to provide a single, aggregate, excitation table'; 'greatly reduces the complexity of stringed instrument implementations'); CMJ98 independently confirms ('impulse responses of other parts, such as the body, are preconvolved with the primary excitation and stored in a wavetable'), yielding a KS variant with an explicit input signal. Smith co-invented the technique (ICMC 1993), so source authority is maximal. Caveat verified in-source: strict commutation requires LTI excitation — nonlinear piano hammers need commuted-piano handling (cf. US Patent 5,777,255).

## FINDING 5 [high | 3-0 (claim 8)]
Dual-polarization string modeling is a concrete, cheap realism technique: run TWO string loops per note (horizontal + vertical polarization) and mix their outputs. Slightly mistuning the pair yields natural beating that explicitly 'reduces the synthesizer-like character'; keeping them equal-tuned but with different loop-filter parameters produces the two-stage decay (fast attack decay into long aftersound) characteristic of real pianos. Cost is 2× an already oscillator-cheap loop.
SOURCES: http://users.spa.aalto.fi/vpv/publications/cmj98.pdf
EVIDENCE: Verified verbatim in CMJ98 including the two-stage-decay half via Figure 10b/10c text ('the loop-filter parameters are different, and a two-stage decay is produced'). Physically grounded in Weinreich, 'Coupled piano strings,' JASA 62(6) 1977 (independent corroboration found by verifier), attributed to Jaffe & Smith 1983, and taught in PASP's Coupled Strings chapter — technique remains current in 2016-2021 literature.

## FINDING 6 [high | 3-0 ×3 (merged claims 9+10+11)]
Piano inharmonicity (stiffness B) can be a LIVE, modulatable per-voice parameter: the Rauhala-Välimäki tunable dispersion filter (IEEE Signal Processing Letters 13(5):253-256, 2006) replaces iterative allpass design with closed-form formulas built on Thiran maximally-flat-group-delay allpass design (at large delays the Thiran phase delay matches stiff-string dispersion). Published architecture: a cascade of four second-order allpasses for keys 1-44 and a single second-order allpass for keys 45-88, with all five design constants tabulated per regime (Filter 1: k1=-0.00050469, k2=-0.0064264, k3=-2.8743, C1=0.069618, C2=2.0427); required Thiran DC delay D(Ikey,B)=exp(Cd(B)-Ikey·kd(B)) with log-polynomial fits kd, Cd in B and Ikey the semitone index from A0 (27.5 Hz); coefficient a1=(1-D)/(D+1). Recomputing when B changes costs just 8 additions, 6 multiplications, 1 division, 1 log, and 3 exp calls — cheap enough for per-note or per-knob-turn updates.
SOURCES: https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2006/papers/p_071.pdf ; https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7855aa74d12e0bd4395d9fe78c6ef93d13aa48ea
EVIDENCE: All formulas, Table 1 constants (digit-for-digit), and the op-count verified verbatim in the authors' DAFx-06 companion paper (p_071.pdf), which restates the SPL method as its ref [7]; the authors themselves frame it as 'real-time control over the inharmonicity coefficient.' Caveats verified in-source: op-count is the coefficient-update cost only (per-sample cascade cost separate); D<1 clamps to identity for high keys; a B change also needs a small delay-line retune to hold pitch; accuracy degrades within ±8.39-cent limits near very high cascade orders.

## FINDING 7 [high | 3-0 ×3 (merged claims 12+13+14)]
Abel & Smith's robust dispersion-filter design (DAFx-06) is the alternative for arbitrary/high-accuracy dispersion: segment the desired group-delay curve into frequency bands each containing exactly 2π area (any stable allpass pole-zero pair contributes exactly 2π to the group-delay integral around the unit circle), assign one first-order complex allpass per band, combine conjugates into real biquads; pole frequency = band midpoint, pole radius in closed form from a user-supplied band-edge overlap fraction β. Design costs less than running the filter, model order is determined automatically by the delay curve, and stable biquad coefficients can be linearly interpolated between designs without unstable intermediates — enabling live retuning. Concrete budgets: ~80 biquads model a low piano note's dispersive delay to 24 kHz, but only ~20 biquads to 6 kHz, and it is (typically) most cost-effective to tune only the lowest-frequency partials correctly (citing Rocchesso-Scalcon).
SOURCES: https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2006/papers/p_013.pdf
EVIDENCE: Full PDF read by verifiers; every element matches verbatim including Eq.(8) θ=(ω++ω-)/2, Eqs.(9)-(11) ρ(β)=η-√(η²-1) with η=(1-β·cosΔ)/(1-β), the 'filter design is less costly than its implementation' statement, and the Figure 10 biquad counts. Biquad-interpolation stability independently corroborated via the convex biquad stability-triangle result. Section 5 fits measured piano note F1 with 64 biquads. Follow-up literature (Rauhala-Välimäki 2006; Abel-Välimäki-Smith 2010) builds on rather than contradicts it.

## FINDING 8 [high | 3-0 (claim 17)]
Digital waveguides (Julius Smith, mid-1980s) model 1D linear media as two non-interacting traveling waves in directional delay lines whose propagation requires 'no computer arithmetic at all'; excitation nonlinearities (bow, hammer, reed) are modeled as lumped elements attached via power-conserving scattering junctions. The paradigm is commercially proven (Yamaha VL1), ships as modules in STK, Csound, and Max/MSP, and was described by Bilbao (2009) — an FDTD advocate, i.e., a concession against interest — as the state of the art in physical modeling, applicable to many strings and most woodwind and brass instruments. This is the default paradigm for the engine's 1D families.
SOURCES: https://www2.ph.ed.ac.uk/~sbilbao/0470510463.pdf
EVIDENCE: Verified verbatim in the genuine Wiley sample chapter of Bilbao, Numerical Sound Synthesis (2009), §1.2.3. Yamaha/Stanford VL1 licensing independently corroborated (CCRMA, Sound on Sound). The one perishable phrase ('state of the art') is explicitly scoped to 2009; a 2026 survey confirms waveguides remain foundational.

## FINDING 9 [high | 3-0 ×2 (merged claims 18+20)]
Modal synthesis is a direct physical interpretation of additive synthesis — an offline eigenvalue solve yields mode frequencies and shapes (stored in a shape matrix), and runtime is a bank of scalar second-order oscillators — but it is essentially restricted to linear time-invariant systems (nonlinear/time-varying behavior needs explicit extensions). The complete struck-object model is y(t) = Σ_n a_nk · e^(−d_n·t) · sin(2π f_n t), where ONLY the per-mode gains a_nk depend on strike/contact location k while frequencies f_n and dampings d_n are fixed object properties. Consequence: a strike-position control is a pure gain-reweight (interpolate precomputed gain arrays), never a mode recompute — directly compatible with Terrain's existing HarmonicEngine partial-bank runtime.
SOURCES: https://www2.ph.ed.ac.uk/~sbilbao/0470510463.pdf ; http://www.persianney.com/kvdoelcsubc/publications/modalpaper.pdf
EVIDENCE: Bilbao §1.2.2/§1.3 verified verbatim ('modal synthesis may be viewed as a direct physical interpretation of additive synthesis ... with minor exceptions, it may only be applied usefully to linear and time-invariant systems'). Van den Doel & Pai's model M={f,d,A} verified: f,d have no location index; A is an N×K gain matrix; their own implementation interpolates only gain arrays at runtime. Corroborated by PASP Modal Expansion and by shipping modal engines (Mutable Elements/Rings position, Chromaphone strike position) working exactly this way.

## FINDING 10 [high | 3-0 ×2 (merged claims 21+23)]
Modal CPU budget is precisely quantified: each mode is a two-pole reson filter v(m) = 2R·cosθ·v(m−1) − R²·v(m−2) + a·R·sinθ·F(m−1), with R = e^(−d/SR), θ = ω/SR, costing only 3 multiplications per mode per sample (reduced from 5 for the complex one-pole form). Measured throughput: a 450 MHz Pentium III ran 800 modes (Java) / ~1000 modes (C) at 22.05 kHz, buffer 128; sorting modes by decreasing importance permits graceful quality degradation under CPU pressure. Implication: per-voice banks of 100-200 modes are trivially affordable on modern CPUs, with importance-sorted culling as a built-in cpu-guard mechanism (mirrors Terrain's existing partial-budget approach).
SOURCES: http://www.persianney.com/kvdoelcsubc/publications/modalpaper.pdf
EVIDENCE: Verifiers downloaded the PDF and confirmed Eq. 6, the 5→3 multiplication derivation, the inner-loop code (exactly 3 multiplies), and the benchmark sentences verbatim; impulse-invariant math independently checked. Caveats: figures are whole-machine throughput at 22.05 kHz (buffer 1 drops capacity to 200 modes); coefficient recompute on retune costs trig; the 'modern CPUs' extrapolation is flagged inference but arithmetically conservative (~100× FLOP growth).

## FINDING 11 [high | 3-0 (claim 22)]
A one-knob Material control is perceptually validated: set per-mode damping d_n = ρ·f_n (damping proportional to modal frequency), where ρ is a single scalar 'internal friction' parameter. Small ρ sounds metallic (long ring); large ρ sounds like plastic or wood (fast damped). Perception studies (Klatzky, Pai & Krotkov 2000, Presence/MIT Press) showed the decay parameter is a powerful determinant of perceived material, outweighing frequency. This is a ready-made ESSENTIAL knob for the 10-control design, and the same frequency-dependent-damping law underlies Material controls in shipping modal products (Chromaphone, Ableton Collision).
SOURCES: http://www.persianney.com/kvdoelcsubc/publications/modalpaper.pdf
EVIDENCE: Quote verified verbatim by PDF download; the cited perception study confirmed real and peer-reviewed. Verifier's strongest qualifier (Giordano & McAdams, JASA 2006): listeners are reliable only at GROSS material categories (metal/glass vs wood/plastic) — which is exactly the axis the knob claims; the source itself hedges that d∝f is 'only approximately valid' in real objects but evokes the material illusion reasonably well.

## FINDING 12 [high | 3-0 (claim 24)]
McIntyre-Schumacher-Woodhouse (JASA 74(5), 1983) provides the canonical universal architecture for ALL sustained instruments: a nonlinear element f = F(q) (reed table, bow-friction curve, or air jet) coupled to a passive linear multimode resonator via convolution with a reflection function r(t) — Eqs. (1),(10),(11): q(t) = q_h(t) + Z·f(t), q_h(t) = r(t)*[q(t) + Z·f(t)]. One identical set of equations simulates the clarinet, violin/bowed-string, and flute families, changing only the nonlinear characteristic F, the reflection function r(t), and parameter values (violin: Z = half the string wave admittance + friction curve with hysteresis rule; flute: delayed nonlinearity f(t)=F{q(t−τ)} plus sign-flipped open-pipe reflection). This is the exciter⊕resonator split a multi-family engine should be built on, extended to brass via PASP's lip-valve/Brasses chapter.
SOURCES: https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~cc/220a/onTheOscillationsOfMusicalInstruments.pdf ; https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/mem2/papers/MUS-ACOUST/mci-schu-woodh-jasa83.pdf ; https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/
EVIDENCE: Verified against the full text (DOI 10.1121/1.390157; original cited CCRMA path 404s, identical file confirmed at ~cc/220a and DAMTP mirrors): abstract quote verbatim; equation numbers/forms exact; §IIC (violin) 'all the model equations ... are the same as before'; §IIE (flute) 'same model equations ... after making one small but crucial modification.' Canonical status corroborated: JASA invited tutorial series; digital waveguide literature and a 2025/26 review credit MSW with cementing the nonlinear-exciter + passive-resonator division; the paper itself anticipates electronic-instrument use.

## FINDING 13 [high | 3-0 ×2 (merged claims 15+19)]
Paradigm selection under a Serum-level CPU budget: physical modeling typically costs hundreds to thousands of arithmetic ops per sample (vs at most ~20 ops/lookups for a bank of six FM oscillators), so the engine must be assembled from the cheap exceptions — KS/SDL loops (~1 oscillator), modal banks (3 mults/mode), and waveguides (free propagation). FDTD (Bilbao/NESS) is the most general approach — uniquely handling strongly nonlinear distributed systems that waveguides and modal synthesis cannot readily model — but its primary disadvantage is numerical instability that must be explicitly engineered against, and Bilbao warns the waveguide efficiency advantage does NOT carry over to 2D/3D systems (waveguide meshes coincide with FDTD schemes). Verdict for Terrain: FDTD is offline/precompute territory (e.g., generating mode data), not a per-voice runtime; membranes/plates/bells should be modal banks, not meshes.
SOURCES: https://www2.ph.ed.ac.uk/~sbilbao/0470510463.pdf
EVIDENCE: Both quotes verified verbatim in Bilbao Ch.1 ('hundreds if not thousands of arithmetic operations ... per sample period'; 'must pay great attention to the problem of numerical instability'; 'the efficiency advantage of the digital waveguide method ... does not carry over to the multidimensional case'). Van Duyne & Smith (ICMC 1993) independently proved 2D waveguide mesh ≡ standard FDTD scheme. Verifier caveats: basic KS is the cheap outlier the hedged 'typically' allows; post-2009 energy-quadratization modal methods handle some nonlinear plates but are not 'readily' general. Per-sample op counts are algorithmic invariants, immune to hardware staleness.
