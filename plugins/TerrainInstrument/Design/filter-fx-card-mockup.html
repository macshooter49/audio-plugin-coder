Trail = the frequency range the cutoff has covered in the last ½ second
What the trail is for. The identity of this device is that the filter moves, and three things move it — the envelope follower, the synced LFO, and key track. The comet behind the cutoff node marks the last half second, so you can see the frequency range the cutoff is actually covering and how fast it is travelling: a slow LFO spreads the dots evenly across the sweep, a percussive follower bunches them and then flings a gap. It answers "is this thing moving, and how far" from across the room.

What it does not do, since I claimed otherwise for a draft and it was not true: the dots ride the curve, so they all sit at the same height and the trail cannot show you which driver is moving the filter. Only spacing distinguishes them. If telling env from LFO at a glance matters to you, say so and I will make age the vertical axis — that version reads as a real time-series, at the cost of putting a time axis inside a frequency plot.

Honest about the audio. This is Web Audio, not our engine — Ladder, Multi, Vowel, Comb, Notches and DJ behave correctly; Acid, Scream and Ring are stand-ins for layout only. The curve is drawn with getFrequencyResponse() on the very node that filters the sound, so what you see is what you hear — the same law the tape card follows.
