These are the real engines. The header pill picks one of the ten groups the synth filter menu already groups by — Ladder · Xpander · State-Variable · Multi · Comb & Reso · Formant · Phase & Diffuse · EQ & Tone · Reverb · Dirty & Weird — and the Engine dropdown picks the engine inside it. All 94 names are read out of the shipped menu, and every one of them fits the pill without truncating (measured, not eyeballed). Char is the shipped drive-type list verbatim: Tube · Diode · Fold · Hard · Crush · Fuzz.

Front mirrors the synth filter exactly — Cutoff · Res · Drive · Mix. The back carries the same engine controls the synth filter has (Env · Track · Spread · Poles · Post) plus the three the envelope follower needs to exist at all (Sense · Attack · Release). Every knob sweeps 0–100; Poles detents to 6/12/18/24 dB and says which, Attack and Release read milliseconds off the same map the audio uses.

One thing I dropped, and you should know: mirroring the synth filter cost the synced LFO (Rate + Sweep). There is no room for it in eight back knobs alongside the engine's own controls, so right now the only thing that moves the cutoff is the envelope follower. If you want the filter to sweep on its own in time with the track, something here has to give — say the word and I will swap a pair out.

Honest about the audio. This is Web Audio, not our engine: lowpass / highpass / bandpass / notch / peaking engines behave correctly, and the comb, ring, bode and bit-crush ones are stand-ins for layout only. The curve is drawn with getFrequencyResponse() on the very node that filters the sound, and the spectrum underneath is the audio actually arriving at it.
